# What is tested, and what the limits are

For what each release added, see [versions.md](versions.md).

| | |
|---|---|
| Tested | Two players on two PCs over the internet: once on 2026-10-31 (they saw each other, formed a party through the console, the menu being empty, fought shared monsters, and damage counted both ways) and a full session on 2026-11-09 on 0.5.0, which produced ten reports; eight are fixed or measured working in 0.6.0 and the last two in 0.6.1. Two game copies on one PC: presence, movement, riding, chat and emotes, trading, following the leader's fast travel, a player's death, and every shared-monster and journal scenario below. Over real Steam, two accounts on one PC (the second in a Sandboxie box): the joiner finds the host's lobby and game traffic flows both ways. |
| Not tested yet | Three or more players (a third game needs more graphics memory than the test PC has). Real encounters beyond one monster nest and one bandit camp (others are spawned by script). |
| Known limits | **A member can watch its leader's cutscenes**, off until a player turns it on (Options, Mods, Witcher Online, "Watch Leader's Cutscenes"). The leader names the scene it entered the moment it starts, and the member plays that same scene in its own world. `CStorySceneSystem.PlayScene` needs one of the scene's inputs by name, and input names are written by hand in the scene resource ("in_first_body", "Input"), so they cannot be guessed: `scene_inputs.py` reads all 6219 of the game's scenes out of its bundles into a table the package carries and the client serves to the scripts (`WO_SceneInputs`). A member tries up to four of a scene's inputs, 1.5 s apart, likeliest first. A try that names an input the leader did not enter at, or a scene whose people that world has not got, starts nothing at all and leaves nothing to undo - which is also what happens when a member has not reached that quest. A member that cannot follow just then (in combat, too far, dead, or the option off) keeps the scene in mind for half a minute and still catches it if it can; one that never manages is told about the cutscene and offered the chance to wait, as before. Only cutscenes are followed, never dialogues - those are each player's own, and their choices already follow the leader's. A member left in a followed scene six seconds after its leader has left skips out of it. The scene plays against the member's own save, so whatever it sets there (facts, journal entries, anything it hands over) is set twice; that is why it is off by default. While a player watches a cutscene the leader's monsters leave that character alone, and the copies around them are held instead of vanishing. **The two worlds keep their own quest state**, so the people standing around can differ. The leader sends the journal twice over: every update as it happens (the last 48), and a snapshot of everything it has reached, 32 entries every 1.5 s with the active quests in the first pages, so a member that joined an hour in catches up instead of keeping whatever its own save said. Both directions are forward only. A member is told by name who the leader can talk to and they cannot, and those people are stood in its world as well, as a sight: you see who your friend is dealing with, but only the player whose quest it is can talk to them. Vendors, quest NPCs and anything else your own save decides stay yours. Copies follow the leader's monsters a few dozen milliseconds behind (most swings 16-56 ms; the water hag's and the bear's about 0.1 s, then caught up). Untested moves: Shaelmaar's charges, the fairytale witch's and the black mage's spells (they never fought in tests), scripted encounter animations. At most 48 monsters are shared at once; a member's own copies of any more stay hidden. Finishers and dismemberment on a shared monster are not mirrored (others see it die normally). Axii's puppet skill (a human fighting for the caster) works in the caster's world only. A mod that replaces the game's effect list, like the Workshop mod "Consumables Balanced", leaves no burning, bleeding or knockdown at all, also without co-op. Quest-fact sync reads the 5.01 game's memory layout and switches itself off if a patch changes it. All ten reports from the 2026-11-09 session are accounted for. The last two: a player being carried was sometimes not seen by the host, which was a passenger that had lost its attachment and was never put back on (`ride_broken` reproduces it and the fix puts it back within a second); and a copy firing a crossbow bolt without raising the bow, which works on this build - the flag goes out every 50 ms, the leader's own crossbow is in its ghost's hand and the ghost plays the aiming pose (`leader_aim`), so that report went with the orphaned copies 0.6.0 fixed. The mod's own text, including its section of the game's options, reads in any language the game runs in. Remastered reads a string table in a format of its own (version 164: the text is UTF-8, one byte a unit, where 4.x used UTF-16), and it decodes each language's file with that language's own key, so the port's 4.x tables - the English one copied under all seventeen names - gave nothing at all outside an English game. `w3strings.py` reads and writes both formats, and `mod_strings.py` writes the mod's eighteen tables from its `en.csv`. **Gwent duels** between players run on the 5.01 Gwent board rebuilt with Witcher Online's duel code, which this package does not include: each player builds it from their own game (WitcherOnline `gwent/README.md`) and installs it with `deploy.py --extra`. The package does include the duel scripts (`gwentDuel.ws`, `gwentHooks.ws`); a player without the built board plays the duel against the AI, and the duel is cancelled when it closes, with no bet paid on either side. What a duel needs is the same Gwent board on both sides, which is the game's own asset and not its exe version: `build.py` decides by the board's sha256. The board of build 5.0.0.1048522 and of Steam's update of 2026-10-29 (5.0.0.1090142) is the same file - that update rewrote the exe and the world bundles and left `r4gui.bundle` alone - so both builds duel, and `build.py build` says which board it found. In Normal mode a player who takes more than 2 minutes over a move cancels the match (no money moves). Duels are started from the console with `duel("<their username>")`. |
